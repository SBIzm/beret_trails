From the Nile River, Egypt to Bosphorus Strait, Türkiye

Travel | Apr 6, 2025

During March of 2026, my girlfriend and I spent three weeks journeying through Egypt and Istanbul, Türkiye. It was a breathtaking experience that took us from the ancient pyramids of Giza to the vibrant, rain-slicked streets of Istanbul.
Our journey began on March 1, 2026, at San Francisco International Airport, where we met two dear friends and boarded a long-haul Turkish Airlines flight bound for the crossroads of continents - Istanbul. We were all awed by the anticipation of exploring world’s two oldest civilizations. After 13 hours in the air, we landed in Istanbul Airport, a modern hub bridging Europe and Asia. At the Istanbul airport, two other friends from Toronto joined our foursome. Later that evening, all six of us continued onward to Cairo — a city blending a thousand years of Islamic architecture with the nearby remnants of Ancient Egypt’s Old Kingdom dating back over 4500 years.


Luxor Temple



Arriving at Cairo airport at night, we were greeted and transferred to the luxurious St. Regis Cairo, set along the legendary Nile River — the lifeblood of Egyptian civilization since antiquity. From our room high above the river, we could see the city lights shimmering on the Nile’s surface, and we could watch traditional feluccas glide past the modern Cairo skyline. It was the perfect sanctuary to retreat after two long flights from San Francisco to Cairo. Later at night, all six of us reviewed our itinerary for the next three weeks over drinks at St Regis.


During the next three days, we visited the world-famous Grand Egyptian Museum, pyramids in Giza and the iconic and mysterious monument, the Sphinx.

We then boarded the Ama Lilia in Luxor for a seven-day cruise down the Nile. Each day brought a new marvel:
The Valley of the Kings: We descended into the spectacular rock-cut tombs of Tutankhamun and Seti I.
Philae Temple: A highlight was visiting this temple dedicated to the goddess Isis, which was moved piece-by-piece by UNESCO to save it from rising dam waters.
The Nubian Village: A colorful motorboat trip introduced us to the friendly descendants of ancient African peoples.
The massive Temple of Karnak and the Temple of Luxor.


Valley Of the Kings



After our Egyptian odyssey, we flew to Istanbul, a city that never fails to enchant, even when the weather turns chilly. We stayed in a familiar apartment in Cihangir, perfectly situated for long walks and local flavors.
During the next ten days we immersed ourselves into Istanbul’s cultural heritage that does not get much publicity as the other world-famous sites like the grand mosques and palaces.
Sakıp Sabancı Museum: Located in the historic "Horse Mansion" in Emirgan, this museum offers world-class calligraphy and stunning Bosphorus views. It’s a peaceful escape from the city's faster pace.
Kariye (Chora): The frescoes and mosaics here are often compared to the Sistine Chapel for their incredible beauty.
Istanbul Islamic Art Museum: a deep dive into islamic art and history
Of course, no trip to Istanbul is complete without navigating these historic Grand Bazaar and Spice market for quilts, fabrics, and tea.
