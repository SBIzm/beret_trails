Waterfront Hike: South from San Francisco Ferry Building to Potrero Point

Travel | Nov 26, 2025

As a lifelong urban hiker, I’ve crisscrossed San Francisco’s coastline countless times, from the iconic trails of the Presidio to the crowded piers of Fisherman’s Wharf. Recently I’ve found that one of San Francisco’s most underrated walks starts at the Ferry Building and heads south along the waterfront to the Potrero Point. , While tourists flock north toward the Golden Gate Bridge, this southbound journey offers a quieter, and a continuously surprising blend of history, working waterfront, modern architecture, and wide-open Bay views.


The Ferry Building, a magnificent Beaux-Arts structure, is the perfect starting line. Completed in 1898, it once served as the city’s primary transportation hub for ferries before the Bay Bridge and Golden Gate Bridge existed. Today it has become a beautiful reboot of itself, housing local food shops and farmers’ markets. The clock tower above you is a nod to a time when maritime travel ruled the Bay.


Recently massive, the monumental sculpture "R-Evolution" by artist Marco Cochrane is installed in Embarcadero Plaza, directly in front of the Ferry Building in San Francisco. It is a 45-foot-tall sculpture made of steel and covered in a stainless-steel mesh.The sculpture was first unveiled at the Burning Man festival in 2015 and it will be there for at least six months.

R-Evolution" by artist Marco Cochrane



When you look east from the Ferry Building, you get wide,open views of San Francisco Bay. Treasure Island and Yerba Buena Island sit in the mid-distance, gently rising from the water.To the right, you can often see the northern tower of the Bay Bridge, with cars streaming across its upper deck.The waterfront is busy with ferries arriving and departing, creating ripples and long wakes on the bay.
Facing south from the Ferry Building gives you one of San Francisco’s iconic urban-waterfront views.The Bay Bridge dominates the skyline, its full western span visible from tower to tower. Just beyond the bridge, the waterfront path curves past Rincon Park, where the giant Cupid’s Span sculpture sits on the grass.



Walking South from Ferry Building to Chase Center


Heading south along the Embarcadero, you transition from the historic financial district edge to the vibrant, modern waterfront. The first big landmark you encounter is the Rincon Center, formerly the Art Deco headquarters of the U.S. Post Office — a reminder that this shoreline wasn’t always about software companies and sleek condos.
Continue walking south and you’ll pass Pier 1 and Pier 3. These once handled cargo from around the world, feeding San Francisco’s rise as a Pacific trading giant. Today, beautifully restored, they host offices, restaurants, and rooftop views. This spot also provides the first of many spectacular, unobstructed views. Looking back, you capture the classic San Francisco skyline — the pyramid, the tower, and the glittering glass — all framed by the Bay Bridge overhead..



Travel Tips

  • Getting to Göcek is a breeze. The nearest major airport is Dalaman Airport (DLM), and from there, you can take a bus or taxi directly to Göcek. The journey is scenic and takes about 20 minutes.
    You can fly to Dalaman Airport directly from many major European airports or from Istanbul Airport


Göcek Marina



Setting Sail from Göcek

Our adventure began in Göcek. We arrived in Göcek from Antalya in the morning and checked into the Alize Hotel in Göcek marina. Göcek is renowned as the yachting capital of Türkiye, hosting several hundred super yachts in its marina. That evening, we enjoyed dinner at a lovely restaurant in the marina.



Travel Tips

  • The best time for a stroll along Göcek Marina is just before sunset. Not only is the light magical for photos, but you can see the locals as well as boaters and tourists from all around the world in restaurants and bars along the marina, making for a lively, authentic atmosphere.


After a night of adjusting to Göcek "dolce vita" lifestyle, next day we boarded our Gület, the Sun World IX. The vessel quickly set sail, gliding south for a short distance before anchoring near a quiet, uninhabited island. Our first evening was marked by an excellent sea bass dinner and introductions to our diverse group of thirteen international travelers as well as our expert crew of five and two guides, one of which was an American archaeologist specializing in maritime history.

Gület SunWorld IX




Islands, Coves, and Coastal Towns

Following a beautiful Turkish breakfast, the Gület continued its journey, pulling into the sheltered waters of Gemiler Köyü, a collection of stunning coves. The water, a perfect 25∘C, was an irresistible invitation for a long swim. After lunch, we hiked Gemiler Island to visit the ruins of the St. Nicholas Church, located at the highest point, offering panoramic views. The sailing took us southeast to the historic town of Kaş. While the town has grown significantly over the years, we were able to explore its rich past, touring a large amphitheater before walking through the streets of the now bustling harbor.

Swimming in Turquoise waters of the Aegean Sea



Good life on Gület



Traveler Tip:

  • Cabins in Gulets are small although they are air-conditioned. If you prefer, you can sleep on matresses laid out on Gulet's deck with beddings provided. Half the passengers in our trip slept on the deck most nights

Exploring Lycian Wonders

A highlight of the voyage was the deep dive into the region's Lycian history. We sailed to Ucagiz, where we encountered spectacular Lycian rock-cut tombs. After a refreshing swim in a beautiful cove, the boat cruised past the protected Kekova Island toward the small village of Kaleköy. We docked and walked uphill to visit the impressive Ottoman Castle, which was built directly on top of older Lycian ruins and even contained a small theater. The evening concluded with a delicious dinner at a local restaurant near the pier.

Lycian rock-cut tomb



Our group enjoying the sunset at Kalekoy Castle theater



Our next stops involved venturing slightly inland from the port of Kalkan. A short van ride took us to the ancient city of Xanthos to explore its expansive ruins. This was followed by a visit to the city of Patara, which boasted larger and better-preserved ruins, including an amphitheater, a bathhouse, and the Lycian League Assembly structure. The size model at the visitor center provided an incredible visualization of the ancient city in its prime. Following a notably rougher-weather sail, the cook prepared a spectacular final dinner: a whole, salt-baked sea bass (levrek).

Gület cook cutting salt-based sea bass (levrek)



Patara amphitheater



Farewell and Fethiye

Our last morning on the Gület began with a peaceful sail to the stunning Butterfly Valley for a final swim before the area became busy. After lunch, we reached our final destination, Fethiye. After docking, we took the opportunity to explore the famous tombs carved high into the hillside and toured the lively Fethiye Old Town. That evening, we enjoyed one last dinner on the Sun World IX before expressing our sincere gratitude to the amazing crew and guides who made the journey unforgettable.



Fethiye's famous tombs carved high into the hillside



Farewell dinner at Fethiye Old Town



Traveler Tip:

  • Book Gület well in advance [3-6 months] online. They fill up quickly

Bidding farewell to the Gület, we transitioned to an apartment in Fethiye. Our post-cruise explorations continued, including a drive to Ölüdeniz for a massive, traditional Turkish breakfast featuring pide bread, sucuk, and kizarmis borek (rolled pastries). The final days were spent relaxing, souvenir shopping, and enjoying the coastal atmosphere with friends, culminating in a traditional Adana Kebab dinner—a perfect end to an incredible Turkish adventure.
