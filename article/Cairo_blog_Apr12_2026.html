Cairo - Gateway to the Nile and "City of Thousand Minarets"

Travel | Apr 12, 2026

As a lifelong urban hiker, I’ve crisscrossed San Francisco’s coastline countless times, from the iconic trails of the Presidio to the crowded piers of Fisherman’s Wharf. Recently I’ve found that one of San Francisco’s most underrated walks starts at the Ferry Building and heads south along the waterfront to the Potrero Point. , While tourists flock north toward the Golden Gate Bridge, this southbound journey offers a quieter, and a continuously surprising blend of history, working waterfront, modern architecture, and wide-open Bay views.


The Ferry Building, a magnificent Beaux-Arts structure, is the perfect starting line. Completed in 1898, it once served as the city’s primary transportation hub for ferries before the Bay Bridge and Golden Gate Bridge existed. Today it has become a beautiful reboot of itself, housing local food shops and farmers’ markets. The clock tower above you is a nod to a time when maritime travel ruled the Bay.


Recently massive, the monumental sculpture "R-Evolution" by artist Marco Cochrane is installed in Embarcadero Plaza, directly in front of the Ferry Building in San Francisco. It is a 45-foot-tall sculpture made of steel and covered in a stainless-steel mesh.The sculpture was first unveiled at the Burning Man festival in 2015 and it will be there for at least six months.

R-Evolution" by artist Marco Cochrane



When you look east from the Ferry Building, you get wide,open views of San Francisco Bay. Treasure Island and Yerba Buena Island sit in the mid-distance, gently rising from the water.To the right, you can often see the northern tower of the Bay Bridge, with cars streaming across its upper deck.The waterfront is busy with ferries arriving and departing, creating ripples and long wakes on the bay.
Facing south from the Ferry Building gives you one of San Francisco’s iconic urban-waterfront views.The Bay Bridge dominates the skyline, its full western span visible from tower to tower. Just beyond the bridge, the waterfront path curves past Rincon Park, where the giant Cupid’s Span sculpture sits on the grass.



Walking South from Ferry Building to Chase Center


Heading south along the Embarcadero, you transition from the historic financial district edge to the vibrant, modern waterfront. The first big landmark you encounter is the Rincon Center, formerly the Art Deco headquarters of the U.S. Post Office — a reminder that this shoreline wasn’t always about software companies and sleek condos.
Continue walking south and you’ll pass Pier 1 and Pier 3. These once handled cargo from around the world, feeding San Francisco’s rise as a Pacific trading giant. Today, beautifully restored, they host offices, restaurants, and rooftop views. This spot also provides the first of many spectacular, unobstructed views. Looking back, you capture the classic San Francisco skyline — the pyramid, the tower, and the glittering glass — all framed by the Bay Bridge overhead.


Continuing south, the walkway curves beautifully into South Beach, leading you directly to Oracle Park, home of the San Francisco Giants. What makes this segment special is the stadium's integration with the water: a public viewing area offers a free glimpse of the game, and the iconic "McCovey Cove" is often dotted with kayakers hoping to snag a home run ball. Even on non-game days, the area is lively but not overwhelming, with views across the water toward the East Bay and the Bay Bridge’s eastern span.


Oracle Park - home of the San Francisco Giants



From there, the path transitions toward Mission Bay, one of the city’s most transformed neighborhoods. Here, old industrial lots have given way to glassy residential towers and biotech hubs. The walk becomes quieter, more open. Make sure to spend a few minutes looking at the fish sculpture installed at Pier 52 Boat Launch area.


Fish Sculpture, Mission Bay



Few blocks past the fish sculpture, the silver, futuristic shape of the Chase Center rises — home of the Golden State Warriors and a symbol of the city’s newest chapter. While it’s undeniably modern, I appreciate how the area around it still prioritizes walkability and public space.

Chase Center - home of the Golden State Warriors



Walking South from Chase Center to to Potrero Point

The true reward of the southbound trail is the final, often-overlooked leg that extends south beyond Chase Center, where fewer tourists wander and the city starts to reveal its working side again. The path along Warm Water Cove at Potrero Point is where things feel almost like a hidden coastal trail. Here, the urban glitter fades, replaced by a working waterfront. You’ll find the remnants of San Francisco’s industrial past, including the massive, historic structures of Pier 70, which once housed the Bethlehem Shipbuilding Corporation Hospital. This area’s piers, built between 1908 and 1938, tell a story of shipbuilding, maritime commerce, cranes, warehouses, and weathered docks.


Takeaway

The views along this southern walk are spectacular but different from the Golden Gate side. Hiking north and west toward the Golden Gate, while iconic, is often a battle against throngs of tourists and the city's notorious wind and fog. The south side offers a different, far more pleasant experience: a calm, sun-drenched walk that chronologically traces the city's growth from its 19th-century maritime peak to its current technological boom. It's a journey not just along the water, but through the city's living history, ending in the quiet, historic industrial landscape of Potrero.



South Waterfront Walk Guide Overview

  • Ferry Building → Oracle Park → Chase Center → Potrero Point
    One-way distance: ~3.8 miles
    Round trip: ~7.6 miles
    Difficulty: Easy, flat, paved waterfront trail
    Estimated time (one way): 1.5–2 hours at a relaxed pace
