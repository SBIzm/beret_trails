Investment opportunities for building and running AI data centers

Markets | December 21, 2025

There are significant opportunities in investing to build and operate data centers due to high demand for computational power. However, there are substantial risks also: such as large capital requirements, intense energy consumption, and supply chain constraints. In a previous Technology post "AI Data Centers - What is the big deal?", AI_Data_Centers, I discussed how AI data centers function. While working on that post, I did some research on investment opportunities in this field. So, in this post, I discuss investment implications and opportunities related to AI data centers.

Reminder: While this analysis provides an overview of market prospects, it does not constitute investment advice.


Investment implications


Massive capital expenditure: Building AI-capable data centers is highly capital-intensive, with McKinsey projecting a need for $5.2 trillion in AI-related data center investment by 2030 [1,9,10,11]. Large tech companies like Meta, Google, Amazon, and Anthropic are planning billions in investments.

Strained power grids and high energy costs: AI data centers consume vast and ever-increasing amounts of electricity, with projections showing U.S. demand potentially multiplying significantly. BloombergNEF [2] projects that U.S. data center power demand could reach 106 gigawatts (GW) by 2035, over a 165% increase from the estimated 40 GW in demand today.This places pressure on existing power grids and contributes to higher energy costs, impacting profitability.

Supply chain bottlenecks: The procurement of specialized AI equipment, such as GPUs (Graphics Processing Units), advanced cooling systems, and backup generators, requires long lead times, which can delay project timelines. [3,4,5]

High competition and potential oversupply: While demand for computing power is strong, intense competition and the sheer scale of investments have led some analysts to raise concerns about a potential bubble and future overbuilt capacity, which could devalue assets [6, 7, 8].

Location and regulatory challenges: Finding sites with sufficient power, and room for expansion is difficult. Data center investment can also be hindered by delays in licensing, permitting, and evolving regulations concerning environment, and data security.


  • How Tech Companies Are Financing the AI-Data-Center Investments

The need for external financing is immense, driving aggressive bond issuance. Since early 2023, large U.S. tech companies have issued more than $120 billion of long-dated bonds. Microsoft, Amazon,Meta and Google have tapped the bond market issuing 10-, 30-, and 40-year notes for building data centers. Typical AI infrastructure requires enormous upfront investment - $5–$10 billion per campus - including power infrastructure. Even cash-rich tech companies prefer to use low-cost capital like bonds rather than erode their balance-sheet flexibility. [12, 13]

Analysts caution that if even 15% of the projected $3 trillion global spending comes to the bond market, it would mean $450 billion of new bond supply. The bond market is thus playing an increasingly critical role in underwriting the AI investments[14, 15]. While public bonds remain the cheapest source of capital, companies are also using private credit, bank construction loans and utility partnerships.


Investment opportunities


Investors can gain exposure to the data center market through several routes:
1- Publicly traded, data center Real estate investment trusts (REITs), such as Equinix (EQIX) and Digital Realty (DLR)
2- ETFs and mutual funds focused on technology and infrastructure
3- Private equity and infrastructure funds
4- Investment in companies that supply the critical AI components such as chips, and power suppliers such as utilities.


Leading AI Stocks and ETFs


Companies in this space are customers and operators of massive cloud data centers, semiconductor and hardware manufacturers. A few examples are:
  Microsoft (MSFT): Runs its Azure cloud platform and has a key investment in OpenAI
  Alphabet (GOOGL): Parent company of Google Cloud, which offers custom Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) alongside GPUs for AI workloads
  Amazon (AMZN): Through Amazon Web Services (AWS), it offers comprehensive cloud services for AI, including its own custom chips
  Meta Platforms (META): Heavily invests in infrastructure to support its AI initiatives and open-source models (Llama)
  NVIDIA (NVDA): The dominant designer of the GPUs
  Advanced Micro Devices (AMD): Another GPU designer and a key competitor to NVIDIA
  Broadcom (AVGO): Another provider of high-speed networking chips
  Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSMC): The world's leading contract chip manufacturer, fabricating the advanced chips designed by Nvidia and AMD
  Vertiv Holdings Co (VRT): A major player in providing AI-ready power and advanced cooling solutions.

Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) such as VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH), Global X Data Center & Digital Infrastructure ETF (DTCR), and Global X Artificial Intelligence & Technology ETF (AIQ) offer a diversified way to invest in AI data centers without picking individual stocks, spreading risk across dozens of companies.


As of early December 2025, stocks and ETFs specializing in AI data centers and related semiconductors have significantly outperformed the broader S&P 500 index. For example while SP500 YTD 2025 return was about 16-17%,while VanEck Semiconductor ETF,(SMH) return was over 42%. The exact YTD returns will vary slightly depending on the close date and the data source.

  • The consensus opinion for 2026 suggests that while leading AI stocks and ETFs are expected to show market growth their relative outperformance compared to the S&P 500 may be less extreme than in 2025 [16, 17, 18].
