Navigating US and Global Market Prospects

Markets | October 22, 2025

As investors assess the financial markets for a path forward, a critical choice emerges: concentrating capital in the robust, often outperforming US markets or seeking the potential diversification and value found in global markets. The current financial landscape, marked by policy uncertainty, geopolitical friction, and shifting monetary dynamics, demands a nuanced perspective on both fronts.
Reminder: While this analysis provides an overview of market prospects, it does not constitute investment advice.


The US Market: Near-Term Momentum vs. Valuation Risks

  • For the next 12 months, the US market is a story of momentum battling increasingly stretched valuations.

The Near-Term Upside (Pros)

The AI Earnings Narrative: The "Magnificent Seven" and other AI-linked firms are still in the driver's seat. Strong corporate earnings tied to this tech revolution are likely to continue supporting US indices like the S&P 500, potentially overwhelming other macro concerns.

The "Soft Landing" Bet: Many investors are betting the Federal Reserve can navigate a "soft landing"—taming inflation without causing a recession. If this happens, it's a massive green light for US stocks, especially mid- and small-caps that have lagged the leaders. Both small-cap and mid-cap stocks have lagged the S&P 500 for the 12-month period ending in October 2025. The underperformance is largely due to the dominance of large-cap technology stocks within the S&P 500, which have fueled the broader index's growth.

Liquidity and Flow: US markets are where the vast majority of institutional money is allocated. Capital tends to flow back to the most liquid and best-performing regions—which is still the US.

The Short-Term Headwinds (Cons)

Valuation Fatigue: The primary risk is that the market is "priced for perfection." If corporate earnings come in just slightly below elevated expectations, or if interest rates stay higher for longer, the market could face a sharp, near-term correction.

Monetary Policy Pivot: The biggest unknown is the timing of Fed rate cuts. If the Fed is forced to keep rates high due to persistent inflation, this is bad news for high-growth stocks whose value is tied to future earnings, making their present valuations look even more expensive.

The Takeaway:

  • The US market has delivered phenomenal returns, but the valuation is demanding a lot from investors over the next year. You're paying for yesterday's growth. International markets are significantly cheaper, meaning they don't need a huge growth miracle—just a small rotation—to generate competitive returns.

Global Markets: The Rotation Candidate

International and Emerging Markets (EM) are a "rotation trade"—a beneficiary when US performance slows or the US dollar weakens. The next 6-12 months could be when that trade gains traction.

The Tactical Advantage (Pros)


  • The Value Play: This is the most compelling short-term argument. With international stocks trading at a deep discount, they offer a margin of safety the US lacks.
    Currency Reversion: Many strategists see the US dollar as overvalued. If this view plays out over the next year, a weakening dollar would instantly boost returns for non-US assets for dollar-based investors, making foreign markets a tactical play.
    China/Europe Stimulus Effect: Any coordinated push for fiscal or monetary stimulus in key non-US regions (like Germany's fiscal push or China's targeted stimulus) could provide a substantial, unexpected short-term boost.

The Near-Term Roadblocks (Cons)

  • Lack of Catalysts: International markets lack the single, overpowering thematic driver (like AI) that the US possesses. They need genuine improvement in the local economic picture, which is currently sluggish in places like Europe.
    Geopolitical Flare-ups: The next year is high-risk globally. Trade disputes or regional conflicts can appear quickly and punish global markets, which are often less liquid than the US.


The Dollar's Critical Role


The weakness of the US Dollar (USD) is a major determinant of international returns. The Bloomberg U.S. Dollar Index fell nearly 10% year to date through September. This may support the continued flow of assets into international equities, since a weaker dollar boosts the value of foreign earnings for U.S.-based investors through currency translation.
The euro to dollar ratio increased from 1.08 to 1.17 during the last 12 months strengthening the euro relative to US dollar. A weaker US Dollar can be a major tailwind for international investments. US investors see a boost in their international returns when the Dollar weakens.

Let me illustrate this Conversion Effect:
Imagine a German company earns €100.
12 months ago, that €100 converted to $107.50 (at 1.075 ratio).
Today, that same €100 converts to $117.00 (at 1.17 ratio).
The US investor automatically gets $9.50 more, simply due to the currency translation. This currency gain is added to any stock price appreciation of the German company, significantly amplifying the total return of the MSCI ACWI ex USA for a US-based investor.

The Takeaway:


  • If you believe the Fed is done hiking and will be cutting rates, you're making a tactical bet against the dollar. A weaker dollar makes your international stock gains look bigger when you convert them back into USD. This currency play is a significant short-term reason to hedge your bets with global exposure.

The 6-to-12 Month Perspective


For the immediate future, the US market is supported by powerful, established trends but is facing an overbought technical picture. Global markets, on the other hand, represent a value-oriented rotation opportunity that is highly sensitive to two factors: slowing US earnings momentum and a weakening US dollar. Factors backing a potentially longer-term slide for the dollar include the large and growing U.S. debt and twin deficits of budget and trade.
An allocation that acknowledges the continued earnings strength in the US, but tactically increases exposure to the cheaper, diversified returns available overseas, may be the most balanced way to play the crosscurrents of the next year.
