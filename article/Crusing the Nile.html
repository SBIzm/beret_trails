Cruising the Nile River - A Journey Through the Heart of Egypt

Travel | Apr 14, 2026

The Nile River is not merely a waterway; it is the lifeblood of Egyptian civilization - acting as the primary source of life, food, and transportation in an otherwise arid desert. Its annual, predictable flooding provided water and rich, fertile silt for agricultural expansion, enabling the cultivation of staple crops like wheat and papyrus. The river also facilitated trade, transportation of building materials, and shaped Egyptian culture, religion, and the calendar.


As I described in my previous posts, 1- From the Nile River, Egypt to Bosphorus Strait, Türkiye , and 2- Cairo - Gateway to the Nile , our Egyptian adventure began in Cairo, where we spent four memorable days immersing ourselves in the nation's profound history. From the sprawling Grand Egyptian Museum and the medieval Citadel of Cairo to the awe-inspiring engineering of the Giza Pyramid Complex, these initial days provided an essential foundation for understanding the millennia of heritage that shaped this land. After four days in Cairo, we continued our exploration of Egypt by embarking on a journey south from Luxor to Aswan, navigating the Nile river aboard the Ama Lilia - river boat operated by AMA Waterways.


Our decision to travel with Ama Waterways for this Nile expedition was a deliberate. We had previously taken a river cruise with them in Europe and were thoroughly impressed by their exceptional customer service, the quality of their side tours, and the overall excellence of the on-board experience. Because of that prior satisfaction, we chose to trust Ama Waterways again among the many travel companies offering cruises on the Nile. The Ama Lilia became our home for seven days as we traversed the river.


Our first morning in Ama Lilia, we got up at 6 am and went up to the top deck to take pictures of sunrise and hot air balloons. After breakfast and then 45 minutes of bus ride from eastern side of the river to the west bank. We first visited Colosi of Memnon, two gigantic statues of Pharaoh Amenhotep III. Then we traveled to Deir el-Bahari to see the Temple of Hatshepsut, an architectural masterpiece built directly into the limestone cliffs by one of Egypt’s few female pharaohs. Then we traveled to west bank of Luxor to the Valley of the King's to see the spectacular rock-cut tombs, of New Kingdom pharaohs, including the famous tomb of Tutankhamun and the highly decorated tomb of Seti I. Valley of the Kings is one of the most famous archeological sites in the world.

That night, as Ama Lilia continued south, we crossed the Esna locks on the Nile. - reminder of the river's controlled power.
The second morning on board Ama Lilia, the major port of call was Edfu,the home of one of Egypt's most remarkably preserved temples - the Temple of Horus. Built during the Ptolemaic period in 230 BC, this is one of the most remarkably preserved temples in Egypt, dedicated to Horus, the falcon-headed God. This Unesco World Heritage site is the second largest temple in Egypt.



Temple of Horus, Edfu


That night’s special dinner on Ama Lilia was wonderful. The menu was all Egyptian food, topped off with a birthday cake for one of dear friends.
Next morning, reaching Aswan, the landscape shifted once more. After breakfast, we took the AMA bus to Philae Temple complex. The temple is in the lake between the old, lower dam (built during British occupation) and the new higher dam built in the 1950s. The Philae Temple Complex, dedicated to the goddess Isis, stands as a triumph of modern preservation. Originally located on an island that submerged following the construction of the Aswan High Dam, the entire complex was meticulously relocated piece-by-piece to a higher island by UNESCO.


Philae Temple, Aswan



After the Philae Temple, we took a motorboat to visit the Nubian village on another island in the lake. Nubian people are descendants of African people and they are very colorful and friendly. Then we took a traditional Egyptian sailboat, felucca, to our ship.



Fellucca - traditional Egyptian sailboat



The following day, there was an optional tour to fly to Lake Nasser to visit Abu Simbel, a UNESCO World Heritage site. We did not take that tour. Instead my girlfriend and I walked to downtown Aswan to see the local markets. Aswan's local markets, feature friendly but often aggressive vendors, driven by economic necessity to secure tourist sales. Sellers follow shoppers, and are very persistent to sell items like spices, fabrics, and souvenirs. A firm "La, Shukran" (No, thank you) and walking away is generally effective. We bought some gifts to take home.
There is a traditional costume party tonight, we will be wearing Galabeya's. A Galabeya party is a popular, festive evening held on many Nile River cruises, where guests and crew wear traditional Egyptian garments, the galabeya. Ama Lilia is heading north to Edfu tonight .



TNext morning, we are cruising north to Luxor. We went through the Esna Locks again which lowered the. boat about 10-15 ft of water. After lunch, we visited the Temple of Karnak, which is the largest religious complex in Egypt. It is a UNESCO World Heritage site. It is built over 1300 years, the complex is simply overwhelming. Its Great Hypostyle Hall, a forest of towering, intricately carved columns, is an awe-inspiring testament to human ambition.
